AI Receptionist vs Hiring: The True Cost Comparison for UK Businesses

The salary on a job advert is only the beginning. Once you account for National Insurance, pension, recruitment fees, sick cover and training, a UK receptionist costs considerably more than most business owners realise — and the comparison with AI is sobering.

The Real Cost of Employing a UK Receptionist

The advertised salary for a full-time receptionist in the UK typically sits between £22,000 and £28,000 per year, depending on location and sector. In London that figure rises further. But salary is only the first line item. Before you can make a rational decision, you need to account for every cost that comes with putting someone on the payroll.

Here is what a mid-range UK receptionist (£25,000 salary) actually costs over a full year:

At the upper end of the salary range (£28,000), total employer cost rises to £36,000–£38,000 per year. And that assumes no management time spent on performance reviews, disciplinaries, or re-hiring when your receptionist leaves — which, in a competitive job market, they likely will.

Worth noting: UK employee turnover in administrative roles runs at roughly 15–20% annually. That means you can expect to go through the full recruitment cycle again within 5 years on average — at a minimum cost of £2,000 each time, before you factor in the productivity gap while the role is vacant.

What an AI Receptionist Can Do

Before dismissing the comparison as too good to be true, it is worth being specific about what an AI receptionist actually handles — because the capability gap between 2024 and 2026 is substantial.

  • Answers inbound calls — 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including evenings and weekends when you or your team are unavailable
  • Routes enquiries — identifies the nature of the call and transfers to the right person, sends a message, or logs the details for follow-up
  • Books appointments — integrates directly with your calendar (Google, Outlook, Calendly) and books slots without any human involvement
  • Handles FAQs — answers questions about opening hours, pricing, location, services, and anything else you configure it to know
  • Sends follow-ups — automatically sends confirmation emails, appointment reminders, or next-step messages after every interaction
  • Captures leads — takes name, number, email and enquiry details from every caller, even the ones who ring at 11pm

For a small business receiving 20–60 inbound calls per week, the majority of those calls are routine: directions, prices, appointment requests, basic questions. An AI receptionist handles all of them instantly, without putting anyone on hold.

What an AI Receptionist Cannot Do (An Honest Assessment)

Any vendor who tells you AI can do everything a human can is selling you something. There are genuine limitations, and you should go in with clear expectations.

  • Complex or escalating complaints — a caller who is genuinely distressed, making a formal complaint, or threatening legal action needs a human. A well-configured AI will recognise the situation and escalate, but the resolution requires empathy and judgement that AI currently cannot replicate reliably
  • Relationship-building with key clients — if you have a small number of high-value clients who expect to speak to a named person, that relationship has real commercial value. Do not route those calls through an AI gatekeeper
  • Nuanced negotiation — pricing discussions, contract amendments, sensitive account management: these belong with a human

The honest framing: AI is exceptional at handling volume efficiently. Humans are better at handling complexity and relationship depth. The question is what proportion of your inbound calls require which.

The Hybrid Approach: Where Most Businesses Land

The most practical deployment for the majority of UK SMEs is not a straight replacement — it is a division of labour. The data consistently shows that approximately 80% of inbound calls to a small business are routine: enquiries, bookings, FAQs, admin. The remaining 20% require genuine human judgement.

In a hybrid model, the AI receptionist handles the 80% — instantly, accurately, 24/7 — and the 20% that genuinely requires a human gets transferred with a full call summary already prepared. Your team arrives at each conversation with context, not cold.

The Hidden Cost of Missed Calls

There is one more number that rarely appears in these comparisons: the cost of calls you currently miss. Research by BIA Advisory Services found that 85% of callers who cannot reach a business will not call back — they move on to a competitor.

If your business receives 30 calls per week and you miss 20% of them (a conservative estimate for a small team), that is six missed opportunities every week. At even a modest average job value of £200, that is £1,200 in potential revenue lost weekly — over £60,000 annually — because nobody picked up the phone.

An AI receptionist does not miss calls. Ever.

Is an AI Receptionist Right for Your Business?

It is a strong fit if you:

  • Receive regular inbound calls that interrupt your working day
  • Lose leads because calls go to voicemail outside business hours
  • Have straightforward booking or enquiry processes that follow a predictable script
  • Are growing and not yet at the stage where a full-time hire makes financial sense

It may need supplementing if you:

  • Work in a sector where caller relationships are deeply personal (private medical, legal, high-net-worth financial advice)
  • Handle a high volume of complex or sensitive complaints that require immediate human empathy

For most UK small businesses — trades, clinics, accountancy practices, letting agents, consultancies — the AI receptionist case is not even close. The economics are overwhelming, the capability is sufficient, and the risk of doing nothing (missed calls, lost leads, 24/7 gaps in coverage) is the biggest cost of all.
